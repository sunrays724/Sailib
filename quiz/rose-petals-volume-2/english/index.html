Not in Vol. 2 — the cheque-book analogy you asked about

The cheque-book analogy is not in Rose Petals Vol. 2, and it is not in any of the 2012 Telugu editions either — the words “checkbook / check book / cheque book” occur nowhere in Vol. 2 (all 5,450 lines), in any of the 12 monthly English 2012 editions, or in the 2012 Telugu editions. So it is not in the question bank below. It does exist — here is where, and what Guruji actually said.

Where: Rose Petals Vol. 1, Chapter 9, “Concretizing Fulfilment” (September 2011 edition), printed page 69. Guruji was asked: “Guruji, would you explain what you mean by ‘concretization of the abstract’?” It is repeated in Vol. 3, page 151.

What it means: the one lakh rupees already on deposit is the abstract fulfilment — the grace already given. The cheque book is the means of drawing it out into your hand, into lived experience. That drawing-out is concretization. The passage says nothing about Baba beyond this, so no further link is claimed here.

Suppose you know that someone has deposited one lakh rupees in your bank account, but you don't have a cheque book and you can't withdraw it – do you think you are rich or poor? You are rich, but you need a cheque book in order to concretize it, to actually get hold of the rupees. The fact that there's one lakh rupees on deposit is abstract; getting them in your hand by withdrawal – that is concretization.
Rose Petals Vol. 1, Ch. 9 “Concretizing Fulfilment” (September 2011), printed p. 69; repeated in Vol. 3, p. 151.

Arati January 2012 · book pp. 3–14
6 teachings · 4 analogies · 5 one-liners

Arati is not a ritual obligation but an expression of love. Guruji reads Dasganu Maharaj's aratis - above all Kakad Arati Padhs X-XI, 'Sai rahama nazara karanaa' - as the voice of a devotee so emptied of ego that he asks only to be the broom (jharu) in Baba's masjid, collecting the dust of devotees' feet. Arati has no inherent value; its value is the way you express it and how it makes you feel. Even silent, happy listening is participating, and the company of co-lovers (fellow lovers of Baba) itself wets a dry mind with love.

Key teachings

Arati has no inherent value; its only value is the expression and the feeling it gives.

It is just an expression, there is no inherent value in it. The only inherent value is the way you express it and how it makes you feel.
p. 8

The real arati stupefies the mind: singing should leave you dumbfounded, forgetting yourself.

And, at least to some extent, when we sing the arati our minds should get stupefied: that is the real arati.
p. 5

Dasganu, an orthodox Brahmin, asked only to be the broom in Baba's masjid, collecting the dust of devotees' feet.

It is enough for me that I collect the dust of your devotees' feet. This is my puja and I'm happy with that.
p. 6

Baba is a companion from beginning to end, not a last-minute rescuer.

Actually, not only 'to the end' or 'at the climax', but 'from beginning to end' - he will be there. Not like the police in a Telugu movie who will come in the last scene, but who won't be of any help, of course!
p. 8

Just listening happily is participating in arati; nobody has to sing.

But just sitting there and happily enjoying listening to it and looking at Baba with respect, that is participating.
p. 9

Being among co-lovers multiplies joy: seeing your own Beloved loved by so many.

When one object of love is loved by so many people, then the whole expression of that love is everywhere and that gives an added enjoyment.
p. 10

The Aesthetic Experience February 2012 · book pp. 15–24
5 teachings · 5 analogies · 7 one-liners

Beauty is not in artworks outside us but in our own life, mind and body: each person is a piece of art more beautiful than Michelangelo's. Aesthetic experience (rasanubhuti) is feeling one with something and forgetting yourself - as when a tragedy makes you weep with joy. Scriptures are the rishis' ecstatic utterances, art not science; life itself is a novel written by Baba, to be read and enjoyed, not a textbook to be annotated. Paradox is a beautiful element of poetry, and the Sadguru's greatness makes us feel small without belittling us.

Key teachings

Your own life is the artwork; be a Michelangelo of your own experience rather than chasing outside art.

I prefer that instead of going for the works of Michelangelo, everybody tries to be a Michelangelo and experiences their own art.
p. 16

Scriptures are expressions of ecstatic love, not dry science; that which affects the heart is real art.

All the scriptures are expressions of love. Try to see that.
p. 17

Life is a novel written by Baba, not a textbook; read it and enjoy it.

This life is a novel, a beautiful novel! Written by whom? Written by Baba. He is the author. Try to read it and enjoy it - don't make it a textbook!
p. 18

Aesthetic experience is oneness with the object, forgetting yourself.

Aesthetic experience is where you feel one with something, you forget yourself. In Sanskrit it's called rasanubhuti, aesthetics.
p. 19

The guru's greatness makes us feel small, but that does not belittle us - we enjoy it; that is the paradox of the Sadguru relationship.

That enjoyment, the aesthetics of it, is the relationship between us and our Sadguru.
p. 22

Start From Where You Are March 2012 · book pp. 25–32
6 teachings · 2 analogies · 5 one-liners

Start from your actual reality - what you want, what is missing - not from grand impersonal goals like mukti or killing the ego, which are tall talk, delusion and self-hypnotization. Temporary happiness (the bird in the hand) is real; let the path be happy too, and let temporary happiness expand into lasting happiness. Your goal and path must be your own personal ones: your nirvana, not the Buddha's. Guruji points to no goal; he only asks you to know your own direction.

First, know what you want. Then that becomes the reference point to which all our life is moulded.
p. 25

So let the path also be happy, not only the goal! Why should the path to happiness be one of suffering? Let the path to happiness be also happy.
p. 26

It may be beautiful to listen to, but 'erasing desires' is another dangerous concept in the idea of lasting happiness because it is not true for us.
p. 27

Start from where you are, from the reality of your own experience, and accept nothing else.

First, know your own reality. Accept your own reality, accept nothing else but your own experience - however profound a concept it may be, or whoever said it.

But that nirvana should be your nirvana, your personal nirvana, not the Buddha's nirvana, not Sai Baba's nirvana nor Ramana Maharshi's nirvana - it should be your own nirvana!
p. 29

Guruji points to no goal for anyone; he only asks you to know your own direction.

I am not pointing you in any direction. I am asking you to know your own direction, what your goal is, and then go in that direction.

In the Orbit of the Magnet April 2012 · book pp. 33–44
6 teachings · 5 analogies · 4 one-liners

The seeker is a piece of iron, the Sadguru a magnet: same material, but the magnet's molecules are ordered and polarized while the iron's pulls conflict and cancel out. Repeated contact with the magnet polarizes the iron without adding or removing a single molecule - that is grace. What transforms the iron is not the magnet but its field, experienced by us as love. Our only effort is to put ourselves in contact, as clothes are put in the sun to dry. Being in the orbit is enough; eventually the iron merges with the sun.

Key teachings

Iron and magnet differ only in order: the magnet's molecules are polarized and aligned; the iron's pulls conflict.

In the magnet all the molecules are in order, properly polarized, and every molecule has a north pole and a south pole. In the piece of iron, the molecules are not polarized - there are no poles and no order, so each molecule's magnetic field is counteracted, or resisted, by another molecule, yet potentially, all the iron molecules are magnets.
p. 33

Contact with the Sadguru polarizes us without adding or losing anything - that is grace.

They will be exactly the same number but their direction will be changed, their attitude will be changed, they'll become properly polarized, harmonized, and they will be aligned and put in order.
p. 34

What transforms the iron is the magnetic field, not the magnet itself - and we experience the field as love.

The real contact is to be in the magnetic field. What transforms a piece of iron into a magnet is not the actual magnet, it's the field.
p. 39

Magnetism is not given by the Sadguru; it is already in the iron - he only awakens it.

Magnetism is not given. It is already there in the piece of iron, so he only awakens it.
p. 37

Baba is our magnet; Ramana called Arunachala a magnetic mountain and stuck to it like iron.

Baba is our magnet. Try to be with him mentally, and if possible, physically, in close proximity - the touch!
p. 38

Being in the orbit of the magnet is enough; the magnet takes care of the rest.

It's enough. Being in the orbit is enough. He'll take care of the rest.
p. 42

Niyama May 2012 · book pp. 45–56
6 teachings · 2 analogies · 6 one-liners

Niyama is a regulation - consistency in a good action - whose value is that it does not let your own mind cheat you of your fulfilment. The mind begins with a noble excuse (meditate at home instead of satsang) and slides within days to sleep, movies and chitchat. Guruji's own niyama: in 1971 his Master told him to take charge of the Thursday satsang at Vidyanagar, and for six years he never missed once - walking 6 km through floods and cyclones, with 104-105 fever, and punishing the mind's tricks the next day (cold bath at 2:45 a.m., walking twice when he missed a bus). Strength comes from the desire to reach the goal; the niyama was not from family conditioning but experienced as helpful, and became spontaneous.

Key teachings

Niyama is consistency in a good action; it stops the mind cheating you of your fulfilment.

It doesn't allow you to be cheated by your own mind, because it keeps you to the goal, to the purpose, so you are more oriented towards your goal.
p. 45

The mind's trick starts nobly - 'I want to sit at home in meditation' - then slides to sleep, movie, chitchat, and satsang is gone.

First it starts like that. The second day our resolve loses some more strength. It's a trick of the mind to avoid going to satsang.
p. 46

Niyama means what you are supposed to do - a regulation, a code of conduct (yogasastras).

Niyama means - what you are supposed to do, a regulation.
p. 46

Guruji kept the Thursday satsang niyama for six years without missing once, whatever the weather or fever.

That is a niyama: I didn't miss doing it, not even once. If floods were expected, I used to go earlier, at five o'clock, and take a raft and then reach Vidyanagar.
p. 48

Strength to hold a niyama comes from the desire to reach the goal.

I don't know where from, but I think that it is from our desire to reach the goal. Only that can give the strength.
p. 55

Guruji judges people by what they do, not what they say.

That's why I never believe what people say, I always take what they do.
p. 55

Light on Anger June 2012 · book pp. 57–66
5 teachings · 3 analogies · 6 one-liners

Ego is what we think we are in the eyes of others - more precisely, what we think others think of us. Anger springs when that image is disturbed, and has three causes: (1) remembering or anticipating something that affects our image (imaginary), (2) misunderstanding, lack of clarity, (3) pain or discomfort (real). The blind man who steps on your toe shows it is not pain but the expectation of politeness that hurts; a child jumping on your chest causes pain but no anger, because there is love. The remedy is clarity for the first two causes and love for the third; ultimately only love kindled by a Sadguru lets us see ignorance instead of evil.

Key teachings

Ego is the image of what we think we are in the eyes of others - what we think others think of us.

Normally in our experience, ego is what we think we are in the eyes of others; it's not an exact definition, but it gives us some idea.
p. 57

Anger has three causes: image-disturbing memory/anticipation, misunderstanding, and pain/discomfort.

Grossly speaking, these are the three reasons for anger. If you think of any of the instances when you get angry, it will be one of these three.
p. 58

Our anger proves our self-image is false: we expect respect and love that is not forthcoming.

Our anger shows that the image we have of ourselves, of what others think of us, is false.
p. 61

Understanding the mechanism behind anger makes it cease, then slowly vanish.

If we can understand the mechanism behind our anger, it will cease. It may come back, but again if we can understand it, it will slowly vanish.
p. 61

Only love kindled by a Sadguru lets us see ignorance, not evil - as Jesus forgave from the cross and Baba forgave like a parent.

Only by having the love kindled in your heart by a Sadguru. Once you have the love in you then you don't see evil; you can see only ignorance.
p. 64

The Chemistry of Love July 2012 · book pp. 67–80
6 teachings · 5 analogies · 8 one-liners

Love - not bhakti as a doctrine, but love itself - is the source, the key and the driving force: Ramana left home out of love for Arunachala, Buddha out of love for solving life's mystery. Right effort is simply an expression of love; effort does not produce the result - grace does, and grace depends on need, not effort. The ego is not to be killed but merges into love like fuel burnt in a flame (the Sadguru's form is the wick). Even 10% love is good, like one iddly to a hungry man. Love grows only in a congenial environment, which you must create - as Himalayan farmers terrace slopes to grow rice. The path is happiness from start to finish.

Key teachings

Love is the key and the driving force of every genuine seeking.

So love is the driving force. It happens in different ways in different people.
p. 68

Ramana left home out of love for Arunachala, not renunciation, maya or a wish for mukti.

What actually drove him from Madurai to Arunachala was simply his love of Arunachala, that's all.
p. 68

Right effort is an expression of love; loving the method more than its object puts the cart before the horse.

The correct effort is always experienced as an expression of our love.
p. 69

The ego is not killed or destroyed; in love it merges, is burnt up as fuel, and loses its identity.

Instead, what I say is, when you experience love, the ego gradually merges into it and becomes one with that love. It gets totally merged and absorbed in it, and loses its identity and form.
p. 71

Effort does not bring the result; grace does - and grace depends on need, not effort.

Our effort - our so-called effort - is an expression of our need, our love, and we can't help it, we have to do it. And by expressing and expressing our need more and more, our love becomes more, it becomes stronger.
p. 74

There is no technique to make love grow; give it a congenial environment and it grows on its own.

There is no method or technique to make love grow. The only thing is, when you realize that you have love and you have a need for love, and you want to realize that love, then try to create or be in an environment where that love is widely cultivated. Then it will grow on its own.
p. 76
